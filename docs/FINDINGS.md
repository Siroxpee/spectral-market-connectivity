# Findings and scope

## Original equities: a weak-node diagnostic

The locally recovered 503-symbol daily panel reproduces 2,643 60-return windows from a 2016–2026 price archive. Raw positive-Laplacian lambda2 lies within 1% of the singleton Rayleigh bound in 84.4% of windows. At the selected March 2020 low, one saved series carries 99.79% of Fiedler squared mass, while mean pair correlation is high. A bottleneck score is therefore not automatically an aggregate fear index. Source artifacts and changing composition remain possible contributors.

## A fixed, more varied ETF cohort

The twelve funds are XLB/XLE/XLF/XLI/XLK/XLP/XLU/XLV/XLY/GLD/TLT/DBC. The first 1,000 common returns, February 7, 2006–January 27, 2010, give a mean correlation of 0.7072 across the 36 stock-sector pairs. TLT's nine sector correlations average -0.3205. These are descriptive period-specific relationships, not permanent hedge guarantees. DBC is a commodity futures fund with collateral/roll exposure, not a spot commodity assessment.

## Primary chronological connectivity comparison

The frozen ETF protocol evaluates 48 main W/H/channel/target cases and two literal negative-Laplacian diagnostics. The [complete comparison table](../output/market_connectivity/comparison.csv) includes every case and five models, rather than a selected best result.

For W60/H20 future averages, 1,241 matched dates span September 30, 2021–September 10, 2026. Added-history MAE is 0.016729 versus 0.018506 no change and 0.015966 current state for positive lambda2/12. Opposing-strength MAE is 0.006159 versus 0.006672 no change and 0.006227 current state. Both primary uncertainty comparisons include no improvement. Nominal 90% interval coverage is 83.2% and 89.0%.

The experiment predicts future graph measurements, not portfolio returns, losses or profitable trades. It has not established a dependable incremental benefit from score history. W/H define different targets, and there is no universal best window. Retrospective market/cohort selection, overlap, calibration drift and fifty-case multiplicity restrict inference.

## Electricity and energy are different research objects

Saved hourly first-difference screens show strong within-market price co-movement: median mean pair correlation is 0.8242 for eleven NYISO zones (2020–2026), 0.8893 for six CAISO hubs/DLAPs (2023–2026), and 0.9775 for four ERCOT hubs (2020–2026). Their different nodes/spans/steps prevent a market-efficiency ranking. Retail monthly total-sector prices across fifty states plus DC instead have median mean pair correlation 0.1921 over 379 five-year windows, 1995–July 2026. These are different price definitions and frequencies, not causal evidence about regulation.

The available long daily wholesale electricity product cohorts have no complete 120-observation windows under strict fixed-cohort missing-data rules. An observed-row futures variant has 4,055 usable W120 windows, but contract/roll mapping remains unverified. These limits motivated the ETF primary experiment rather than silently filling or splicing products.

## What remains untested

- Prospective forecasts of a concrete market-risk target such as volatility, tail loss, covariance or hedge failure beyond simpler baselines.
- An availability-time-aligned oil–gas–power study with region/product identity, appropriate lags, weather/load and physical transfer data.
- Point-in-time universes and corporate actions, independent calendar reconciliation, repeated external validation, execution costs and trading feasibility.

The next experiment should freeze its dataset, target, information cutoff and evaluation before new outcomes arrive. Negative and uncertain results remain part of the research record.
